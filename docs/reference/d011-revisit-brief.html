The doctrine has been measured. Now what.

D-011's revisit trigger fired on 25 July. This is the brief for the ruling: what the three studies found, what the evidence can and cannot settle, five available positions with what each changes on Monday morning, and a recommendation.

1 · Three questions were asked. Three answers came back.

Each study narrowed the claim the doctrine could still make. Read them in order — the last one is the one nobody expected to have to write.

2 · Why nothing separates — the picture that carries the ruling

Each bar is one arm's Sharpe under 50 random selections from its own admitted set — the range you'd get by picking differently. The dot is where the system actually landed using score-order. The shaded column is where all three benchmarks sit.

The bands are wide because selection is powerful, not because the system is futile. S1's range runs from 0.09 to 0.97 — a spread produced entirely by changing which admitted name you buy, with the gate held fixed. An instrument with that much variance cannot resolve gate-sized differences. "Inside the band" is therefore a statement about the experiment's resolution at least as much as about the doctrine.

3 · Where the edge actually lives

Four layers make entry decisions. The evidence grades them very differently, and the two that survive are not the two that got argued about.

Recommendation: B, explicitly interim. Three reasons. (1) The evidence genuinely supports no direction — every option above except B requires acting on a difference the study could not resolve. (2) Sequencing: ordering is the larger measured lever, and loosening the gate enlarges the surface where an unspecified ordering rule operates. Settle the bigger lever first. (3) The real hazard isn't the gate — it's believing the chip. Layer A says an A+ chip does not mean "this will do well." Correcting that belief costs nothing and removes the actual danger.

What B ships: no code change to the gate; the doctrine record amended to state that the grade has no demonstrated forward-return edge and functions as a risk-shape filter; the close report and hover text reworded to match; the standing rule kept that any A+ appearing before the selection ruling comes to deliberation rather than being entered on the chip alone; and an explicit expiry — B lapses when the selection rule is ruled, at which point A and C are reconsidered on the new evidence.

What this brief does not settle

Unresolved is not disproven. The study could not distinguish the arms; that is a limit of an instrument with enormous selection variance over one window, not a demonstration that the gate is worthless.

The pooled ordering evidence is weaker than its p-values. The five arms share days and heavily overlapping candidate sets — five dependent looks at one window, not five trials. Direction consistent, magnitude large, joint significance not honestly quantifiable from this design.

The ordering prescription is metric-dependent. Native beats score 4/4 on expectancy and 1/4 on Sharpe; low-extension picking earns expectancy while degrading the curve. "Which ordering is best" has no single answer in this sample.

One macro era, one window. 2020–2026 rewarded momentum. The inversion held in both halves including the 2022 bear, which limits but does not eliminate the caveat.

A separate live finding, not part of this ruling: roughly 32–37% of stop exits filled below the entry stop. Realized risk exceeds 1R about a third of the time, which is a position-sizing input and deserves its own item.
