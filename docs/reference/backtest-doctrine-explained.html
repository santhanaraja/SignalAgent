1 · What one row is — and the wall that keeps it honest

The study's atom is a ticker-day: one stock, one trading day, graded using only what was knowable at that day's close, then joined to what happened afterwards. Everything hangs on the two halves never touching.

This is why a chart eyeball won't match the printed number. The grade is struck on day D's close, but the return starts at the next open — because that is the earliest moment you could actually have bought. That's the D-006 fill discipline. Measuring from D's close would credit the study with a fill nobody could have gotten, and would flatter every bucket by roughly one overnight gap.

2 · What was graded — and the two rows that had to go

The production grade has seven rows. Five replay perfectly from price history. Two depend on artifacts that simply don't exist before July 2026, so they were deleted rather than guessed — and the deletion's cost was then measured instead of assumed.

The None-split is the detail that stops this study from repeating a production bug. Production auto-passes an unknown earnings runway — a missing date reads as "clear." In the backtest that would have let every coverage-gap name silently ace row 7 and inflate the A+ bucket. So None was split in two: a company with genuinely no scheduled print passes; a company whose date simply couldn't be fetched can never be A+. Same disease as the fabricated breaker, caught before it reached the evidence.

Read the spread, not the levels. The population is today's pool, so absolute returns carry survivorship bias — but that bias inflates every bucket alike. The comparison between buckets is what survives it, and the comparison is what the doctrine actually claims.

What this study does not prove

It measures the signal, not the system. No stops, no sizing, no exits, no profit targets. An A+ entry sits within 1.8×ATR of a rising SMA20, so its stop is close and being wrong is cheap; an extended leader's isn't. Return per unit of risk is the tradable metric, and that is Build 5B's question — the doctrine's remaining case lives entirely there.

The Feb-2020 instances sharpen that, rather than softening it. All five worst A+ days fall days before the COVID crash. Row 6 was deleted — but no breaker was firing on 19 February either; breakers fire on stress. What would have defended those entries is the regime chassis downgrading and the stops firing, both of which are strategy-layer machinery this study deliberately doesn't model.

One macro era. 2020–2026 rewarded momentum. The force of this caveat is limited by the inversion holding in both halves — including the 2022 bear — but it is not eliminated.
